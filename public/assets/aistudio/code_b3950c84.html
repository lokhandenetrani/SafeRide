Ground Reality & Need Analysis

A Critical Gap in Rider Safety

In India, two-wheelers represent over 70% of registered vehicles. While helmet legislation exists, daily commuter observations and verified accident reports reveal systemic gaps between wearing a helmet and receiving timely medical support.

Ministry of Road Transport (MoRTH)
50,029
Two-Wheeler Rider Fatalities

Two-wheeler riders accounted for 44.5% of total road fatalities across the nation in 2022, marking the most vulnerable commuter segment.

Source: MoRTH Road Accidents in India (2022 Report)
World Health Organization
~42%
Reduction in Fatal Injury Risk

Correct helmet fastening reduces fatal head trauma by over 40%, yet unbuckled straps remain prevalent during quick commutes and delivery routes.

Source: WHO Global Status Report on Road Safety
Emergency Response Window
The "Golden Hour"
Crucial Post-Crash Medical Window

Most rural or nighttime collisions lack immediate third-party reporting. Crucial minutes are lost before emergency responders learn the crash location.

Source: National Health Systems Resource Centre (NHSRC)

Primary Roadblocks Identified in Student Field Research

01 / HABIT

Improper Strap Use

Riders frequently wear helmets resting unfastened to avoid traffic fines, causing the helmet to eject during an initial impact.

02 / ISOLATION

No Crash Awareness

Conventional helmets are passive polycarbonate shells incapable of recognizing rapid deceleration or rollover shocks.

03 / DELAY

Emergency Response Lag

If a rider is incapacitated off the highway or during late hours, contacting emergency contacts or ambulance services is severely delayed.

04 / BARRIER

Prohibitive Cost

Commercial smart helmets retail between ₹8,000–₹25,000, completely out of reach for average Indian students and gig delivery riders.

05 / USABILITY

Weight & Ergonomics

Cumbersome built-in gadgets make helmets heavy, hot, and difficult to sanitize. An external modular add-on solves this limitation.

Non-Invasive Architecture

Introducing a Smarter Layer of Protection

Rather than asking millions of riders to discard their ISI-certified helmets, SMARTHELM proposes a lightweight modular retrofit unit that secures to existing chin-straps and helmet shells.

Strap Fastening Detection

Uses a conductive or tactile micro-limit switch embedded into the chin buckle mechanism. Verifies continuous contact before acknowledging safe riding state.

Hardware Breakdown & BOM

Small Components. Meaningful Potential.

By choosing ubiquitous, open-source embedded electronics, the proposed design avoids proprietary silicon markups while ensuring repairability and student-accessible experimentation.

MCU

ESP-WROOM-32

Dual-Core 240MHz SoC

Acts as the central processing unit. Reads sensor buses, handles time-critical debouncing, manages deep sleep power states, and hosts BLE communication.

Bus: I2C / GPIO Est. Cost: ~₹280–350
IMU

MPU-6050 / ADXL345

6-Axis Motion Sensor

Measures angular rate and 3-axis acceleration. Calibrated with interrupt thresholds to distinguish normal head tilting from sudden high-impact collisions.

Range: ±16g Est. Cost: ~₹120–180
SW

Tactile Chin-Strap Interlock

Micro-Limit / Magnetic Reed

Integrated into the standard double-D ring or micrometric clasp. Ensures the circuit closes only when the buckle is physically latched with proper tension.

Pull-up Debounced Est. Cost: ~₹30–60
GPS

NEO-6M GPS Receiver

Positioning Hardware (Proposed)

Captures latitude and longitude coordinates. In lightweight configurations, this telemetry can alternatively be delegated to a paired smartphone via BLE.

UART NMEA 9600 Est. Cost: ~₹280–400
COM

SIM800L / Smartphone BLE

Dual-Path Communication

Sends automated SMS dispatch packets. Tested via standalone 2G GSM cellular module and low-power companion app bridge for zero additional SIM subscription cost.

AT Commands / BLE 4.2 Est. Cost: ~₹250–380
PWR

Li-Po & Active Piezo Sounder

3.7V 650mAh & 85dB Warning

Provides 85dB acoustic alert for false alarm countdown. Coupled with a protected lithium pouch cell and TP4056 micro-USB/Type-C charging management board.

Battery: 14h Active Est. Cost: ~₹150–220

Human-Centered Methodology

Built Around the Rider

The project followed the 5-stage Stanford d.school Design Thinking framework to ensure technical decisions originated from genuine commuter habits rather than theoretical assumptions.

STAGE 01

Empathize

Observed college campus bike parking & commuter junctions. Conducted 24 brief rider interviews about helmet habits and safety fears.

Key finding: Buckles left undone for quick errands.
STAGE 02

Define

Framed the core challenge: "How might we create an affordable add-on that nudges proper strap latching and alerts contacts without nuisance alarms?"

Focus: False-alarm resistance is critical.
STAGE 03

Ideate

Brainstormed 14 safety concepts: alcohol sensors, ignition lockouts, full smart visors. Downselected to a modular retrofitted strap & crash beacon.

Selected: Modular retrofit unit.
STAGE 04

Prototype

Breadboard assembly using ESP32, MPU6050, micro-switch, and SIM800L. Modeled PLA enclosure in CAD to test weight and latch ergonomics.

Outcome: Functional breadboard rig.
STAGE 05

Test

Subjected module to simulated drops, running steps, speed bumps, and helmet removal. Tuned threshold algorithms to reject routine drops.

Result: Added 20s grace cancellation.
Rider Interviews (N=24)

"Why is the strap left unfastened?"

62% of surveyed student riders admitted to riding with unbuckled straps on trips under 2 km. The primary reason cited was the hassle of repeated fastening during multiple campus stops.

Insight: Safety cues must be non-intrusive.
Design Heuristic

Why Avoid Motorcycle Ignition Lockouts?

Early ideas explored an RF ignition cut-off if the helmet wasn't worn. However, interviews revealed users bypass or hack ignition interlocks when running late or loaning bikes. A safety nudge approach proved far more acceptable.

Insight: Autonomous alerting beats forced cutoffs.
Prototype Observation

Drop vs Crash Threshold Discrepancy

Dropping a helmet from 1 meter onto asphalt generates high G acceleration spikes comparable to an actual crash. Without a manual abort button and rotational gyroscope cross-checking, false alarms would be unacceptably high.

Insight: Gyro vector cross-checking required.

PURPOSE-DRIVEN INNOVATION

Technology That Looks Beyond the Ride.

Exploring how accessible technology can support better riding habits and faster communication after a potential crash.

1. Nudging Safer Helmet-Use Habits

Rather than punishing users, subtle acoustic and optical feedback reminds riders to click their chin strap before speeding onto crowded Indian roadways. Creating instinctive compliance saves lives.

Goal: 100% fastened rides

2. Democratizing Safety Innovation

Life safety must not be a luxury feature reserved for high-end sportbikes. An open-source, affordable retrofit module brings smart IoT protection to students, food delivery couriers, and working-class families.

Goal: Sub-₹1,500 target accessibility

3. Rapid Emergency Communication

By automatically transmitting latitude and longitude to nominated emergency contacts when an incapacitating collision occurs, the system aims to compress the delay before first-aid discovery.

Goal: Minimizing the Golden Hour gap
Academic Research Integrity: The project does not claim proven reductions in nationwide road fatalities or guaranteed medical rescue outcomes. It is an engineering investigation into closing known communication lags.

Clarity & Specifications

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear technical answers regarding compatibility, reliability, and testing limits.

What is a smart helmet add-on?

Unlike purpose-built smart helmets that require buying a completely new helmet, an add-on is a modular electronic assembly that attaches to existing ISI-certified helmets. It provides intelligent strap verification and crash sensing without modifying the helmet's structural energy-absorbing foam.

How does strap detection work?

A tactile micro-switch or magnetic reed contact is embedded near the chin strap buckle. When the strap is threaded and tightened, the physical tension closes the circuit, signaling to the microcontroller that the helmet is properly fastened.

How is a potential crash identified?

A 3-axis accelerometer and gyroscope (such as the MPU6050) measures instantaneous deceleration. When G-forces exceed configured safety thresholds (typically >4.5G coupled with rotational velocity), the system registers an impact event and enters the confirmation state.

How does the SOS workflow prevent false alarms?

A 20-to-30 second grace period immediately starts when an impact is logged. An 85dB onboard buzzer sounds and an LED flashes. If the rider dropped their helmet accidentally or is uninjured, pressing a physical cancellation toggle instantly resets the system, stopping any SMS transmission.

What is the estimated target cost?

The project targets an add-on hardware bill of materials around ₹1,000–₹1,500. This is an engineering design budget for off-the-shelf components, not a commercial retail price, which would depend on industrial tooling, QA testing, and certifications.

Is the system already fully tested in real crashes?

No. As a student prototype, testing has been strictly conducted on laboratory bench rigs, drop tests with dummy weights, and simulated commuter vibration runs. It is not currently approved for clinical or regulatory crash safety certification.

Can it work with every helmet and smartphone?

Compatibility depends on the specific chin-strap latch mechanism (double-D ring vs quick-release buckle) and cellular network availability (2G GSM or BLE smartphone connectivity). Full universal fit remains a future development objective.
